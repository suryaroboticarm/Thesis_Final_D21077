\chapter{Reinforcement Learning Preliminaries: GAE, the PPO Clipped Objective, and CTDE}
\label{app:rl}

Chapter~\ref{chap:learned_control} builds both of its studies on Generalized Advantage Estimation
(GAE) and the PPO clipped surrogate objective (Equations~\eqref{eq:dikppo_gae}
and~\eqref{eq:dikppo_ppoloss}), and its second study further extends them to a centralized-critic,
decentralized-actor setting (Equations~\eqref{eq:alpha_gae}--\eqref{eq:alpha_totalloss}), all cited
to~\citep{schulman2017proximal,yu2022surprising} rather than derived. This appendix derives GAE from
its underlying $k$-step estimators, motivates the clipped objective as a pessimistic bound on the
unclipped surrogate, and extends both to the centralized critic used in ALPHA.

%% ============================================================
\section{The \texorpdfstring{$k$}{k}-Step Advantage Estimator}\label{sec:appE_kstep}
%% ============================================================

The $k$-step advantage estimator bootstraps $k$ real rewards against the critic's value estimate
thereafter,
\begin{equation}
\hat{A}_t^{(k)} = \sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\gamma^l r_{t+l} + \gamma^k V(s_{t+k}) - V(s_t).
\label{eq:appE_kstep}
\end{equation}
Writing the one-step temporal-difference residual as $\delta_t = r_t + \gamma V(s_{t+1}) - V(s_t)$
and expanding $\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\gamma^l\delta_{t+l}$ term by term,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\gamma^l\delta_{t+l} = \sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\gamma^l r_{t+l}
+ \sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\gamma^{l+1}V(s_{t+l+1}) - \sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\gamma^l V(s_{t+l}),
\label{eq:appE_expand}
\end{equation}
the last two sums telescope, since every $V(s_{t+l})$ term for $1\leq l\leq k-1$ appears once with
coefficient $+\gamma^l$ and once with coefficient $-\gamma^l$, leaving only the first and last
values, $\gamma^kV(s_{t+k}) - V(s_t)$. Equation~\eqref{eq:appE_expand} therefore reduces exactly to
Equation~\eqref{eq:appE_kstep},
\begin{equation}
\hat{A}_t^{(k)} = \sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\gamma^l\delta_{t+l}.
\label{eq:appE_kstepdelta}
\end{equation}

\section{GAE as an Exponentially-Weighted Average}\label{sec:appE_gae}
%% ============================================================

GAE$(\gamma,\lambda)$ is defined as an exponentially-weighted average over every horizon $k \geq 1$,
with weight $(1-\lambda)\lambda^{k-1}$ summing to one over $k$:
\begin{equation}
\hat{A}_t^{\text{GAE}} = (1-\lambda)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{k-1}\hat{A}_t^{(k)}
= (1-\lambda)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{k-1}\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\gamma^l\delta_{t+l},
\label{eq:appE_gaedef}
\end{equation}
using Equation~\eqref{eq:appE_kstepdelta}. Exchanging the order of summation, for a fixed $l \geq 0$
the inner term $\gamma^l\delta_{t+l}$ appears in Equation~\eqref{eq:appE_gaedef} for every $k \geq
l+1$,
\begin{equation}
\hat{A}_t^{\text{GAE}} = (1-\lambda)\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\gamma^l\delta_{t+l}\sum_{k=l+1}^{\infty}\lambda^{k-1}
= (1-\lambda)\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\gamma^l\delta_{t+l}\,\frac{\lambda^l}{1-\lambda}
= \sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(\gamma\lambda)^l\delta_{t+l},
\label{eq:appE_gaeresult}
\end{equation}
using $\sum_{k=l+1}^{\infty}\lambda^{k-1} = \lambda^l/(1-\lambda)$, the tail of a geometric series.
Equation~\eqref{eq:appE_gaeresult} is exactly the (infinite-horizon) form of
Equation~\eqref{eq:dikppo_gae}; the finite sum to $N_{\text{steps}}$ used there is the natural
truncation of Equation~\eqref{eq:appE_gaeresult} to the length of a single collected rollout, with
$\lambda \to 1$ recovering the full Monte Carlo return-based advantage and $\lambda \to 0$ recovering
the one-step estimator $\hat A_t^{(1)} = \delta_t$.

\section{The PPO Clipped Objective as a Pessimistic Bound}\label{sec:appE_clip}
%% ============================================================

The conservative policy iteration objective $L^{\text{CPI}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_t[\rho_t(\theta)\hat
A_t]$, with $\rho_t(\theta) = \pi_\theta(\mathbf{a}_t\mid\mathbf{s}_t)/\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}
(\mathbf{a}_t\mid\mathbf{s}_t)$ as in Equation~\eqref{eq:dikppo_ppoloss}, is only a locally valid
surrogate for the true expected return: it is exact at $\rho_t=1$ (no policy change) but its linear
growth in $\rho_t$ gives no guarantee once $\theta$ moves far from $\theta_{\text{old}}$, since the
importance-sampling estimate it is built from degrades as the two policies diverge. Trust-region
methods bound this divergence with an explicit constraint, $\max_\theta L^{\text{CPI}}(\theta)$
subject to $\mathbb{E}_t[\text{KL}(\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}\Vert\pi_\theta)] \leq \delta$. The
clipped objective of Equation~\eqref{eq:dikppo_ppoloss} achieves a similar effect without an explicit
constraint, by removing the incentive to move $\rho_t$ far from $1$ once its sign-appropriate
direction is already saturated. Consider the two cases of the inner term separately:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\hat A_t > 0$ (a better-than-expected action): $L^{\text{CLIP}}$'s per-sample term is
    $\min\left(\rho_t\hat A_t,\, (1+\epsilon)\hat A_t\right)$, which grows with $\rho_t$ only until
    $\rho_t = 1+\epsilon$, after which the minimum saturates at the constant
    $(1+\epsilon)\hat A_t$. Its derivative with respect to $\rho_t$ is therefore zero for $\rho_t >
    1+\epsilon$, removing the gradient signal that would otherwise keep increasing the action's
    probability without bound.
    \item $\hat A_t < 0$ (a worse-than-expected action): the same minimum instead saturates once
    $\rho_t$ falls below $1-\epsilon$, removing the gradient signal that would keep decreasing the
    action's probability without bound.
\end{itemize}
In both cases $L^{\text{CLIP}}(\theta) \leq L^{\text{CPI}}(\theta)$ term by term, since the minimum of
two quantities never exceeds either, so $L^{\text{CLIP}}$ is a pessimistic (lower) bound on the
surrogate objective that is exact near $\rho_t = 1$ and flattens exactly where the trust-region
constraint would otherwise become active, achieving qualitatively the same restraint on the policy
update as the explicit KL constraint without requiring it to be solved at every step.

\section{Extension to a Centralized Critic (CTDE)}\label{sec:appE_ctde}
%% ============================================================

The Multi-Agent PPO objective of Equations~\eqref{eq:alpha_gae}--\eqref{eq:alpha_totalloss} used for
ALPHA's coordination policy replaces every occurrence of the single-agent state $s_t$ used as the
critic's argument above with the shared global state $S_t \in \mathbb{R}^{92}$, while leaving the
actor's argument as each agent's own local observation $\mathbf{s}_t^i \in \mathbb{R}^{47}$. The
$k$-step telescoping argument of Section~\ref{sec:appE_kstep} and the exponential-weighting argument
of Section~\ref{sec:appE_gae} depend only on $V(\cdot)$ being a scalar function bootstrapped
consistently across consecutive timesteps; substituting $V_{\phi^i}(S_t)$ for $V_\theta(s_t)$
throughout leaves the algebra of Equations~\eqref{eq:appE_expand}--\eqref{eq:appE_gaeresult}
unchanged and yields Equation~\eqref{eq:alpha_gae} directly, with per-agent reward $r_{t+1}^i$
retained (each agent is credited for its own progress) while the bootstrap target is evaluated on the
state both agents' configurations are visible in. This is precisely the asymmetry that defines CTDE:
the actor loss $\mathcal{L}^{\text{CLIP}}$ of Equation~\eqref{eq:alpha_totalloss} is a function of
$\mathbf{s}_t^i$ alone, so the trained policy can be executed with only local information, while the
critic loss $\mathcal{L}^V$ is a function of $S_t$, so training benefits from information no single
agent observes at deployment. Section~\ref{sec:appE_clip}'s clipping argument applies unchanged to
$\rho_t^i(\theta)$ of Equation~\eqref{eq:alpha_ratio}, since it depends only on the ratio of two
policy densities evaluated at the same local observation, not on how the accompanying advantage
estimate was computed.
